## 1. Introduction

This practical test was carried out to understand the correct procedure for disassembling, inspecting, measuring, and assembling an internal combustion engine. The engine used for this practical was the Nissan QG15DE petrol engine.

The main objective of this practical was to identify major engine components, remove them in the correct sequence, inspect them for wear or damage, take important measurements, and reassemble the engine using correct workshop procedures. During the activity, safety, cleanliness, correct tool usage, and manufacturer specifications were followed as important requirements.

### Objective

The objectives of this practical were:

- To prepare a safe and clean workshop area for engine work.
- To identify the main external and internal engine components.
- To disassemble the engine step by step.
- To inspect engine components for cracks, scoring, carbon buildup, and excessive wear.
- To measure important clearances and compare them with specifications.
- To assemble the engine correctly using lubrication, correct alignment, and correct tightening procedures.
- To understand the importance of timing marks, torque specifications, and final engine checks.

## 3. Workspace Preparation

Before starting the disassembly process, the workspace was prepared properly. A clean and organized work area is important because engine components are precision parts and can be damaged by dirt, incorrect storage, or mixing up parts.

The engine was placed securely on an engine stand before work started. The stand allowed safe access to the top, bottom, front, and rear sections of the engine. Tools were arranged before starting, and containers were prepared for bolts, nuts, washers, and small components.

The following preparation steps were followed:

- The work area was cleaned and cleared of unnecessary items.
- The engine stand was checked to confirm it was stable and secure.
- Required tools were arranged before starting the work.
- Containers or trays were prepared to keep removed parts in order.
- Components such as pistons, connecting rods, bearing caps, and bolts were marked or arranged according to cylinder number.
- Waste containers were prepared for used oil and coolant.

## 5. Pre-Disassembly Procedures

Before disassembling the engine, external checks and preparation were completed.

### Fluid Drainage

The engine oil was drained by removing the oil drain plug and allowing the oil to flow into a suitable waste oil container. The coolant was drained from the cooling system into a separate container. Oil and coolant were not allowed to spill onto the floor because this can create a slipping hazard and cause environmental contamination.

### External Component Removal

The following external components were removed before internal engine disassembly:

- Battery terminals were disconnected if the engine was still installed in the vehicle.
- Air intake components were removed.
- Intake manifold was removed or disconnected.
- Exhaust manifold was removed.
- Alternator was removed.
- Water pump and related cooling components were removed as required.
- Electrical connectors were disconnected carefully.
- Fuel lines and vacuum hoses were disconnected and identified.
- Drive belts were removed.
- Spark plugs and ignition coils were removed.

During removal, bolts and nuts were kept in order to make assembly easier.

## 6. Engine Disassembly Procedure

### 6.1 Cylinder Head Removal

The valve cover was removed first to expose the camshafts and valve train area. The No. 1 piston was positioned at Top Dead Center on the compression stroke before timing components were removed.

The timing marks were checked and recorded before the timing chain was removed. This is important because incorrect timing during assembly can cause poor engine operation or valve damage.

The cylinder head bolts were loosened gradually in the correct sequence. The bolts were loosened in two or three stages to prevent cylinder head warping. Removing the cylinder head bolts in the wrong order can cause distortion or cracking.

Main steps:

1. Remove valve cover.
2. Remove ignition coils and spark plugs.
3. Set No. 1 piston at TDC on compression stroke.
4. Mark or record timing chain alignment marks.
5. Remove timing chain components carefully.
6. Loosen cylinder head bolts gradually in the recommended order.
7. Remove the cylinder head from the engine block.
8. Place the cylinder head on a clean surface for inspection.

### 6.2 Timing System Removal

The QG15DE uses a timing chain system. During removal, the timing marks on the chain and sprockets were observed and recorded. The chain tensioner and guides were removed carefully.

Important points:

- Timing marks must be aligned before removal.
- Camshaft and crankshaft should not be rotated separately after removing the chain.
- Chain guides and tensioner must be inspected for wear.
- Timing components should be stored in order.

### 6.3 Bottom End Teardown

After the cylinder head and timing components were removed, the bottom end was disassembled.

Main steps:

1. Remove the oil pan.
2. Remove the oil strainer and oil pump if required.
3. Remove connecting rod caps.
4. Push piston and connecting rod assemblies out carefully.
5. Number each piston and connecting rod according to its cylinder.
6. Remove main bearing caps.
7. Remove the crankshaft.
8. Remove main bearings and thrust bearings.
9. Store bearings and caps in correct order.

It is important that connecting rods, pistons, bearing caps, and bearings are not mixed up because they wear together during engine operation.

## 7. Inspection and Measurement

After disassembly, all components were cleaned and inspected. Carbon buildup, old gasket material, and oil deposits were removed carefully. A scraper and suitable cleaning tools were used, but sealing surfaces were not scratched or damaged.

### Visual Inspection

The following checks were carried out:

- Cylinder head checked for cracks, corrosion, and warping.
- Cylinder block checked for cracks, scoring, and damaged threads.
- Cylinder walls checked for scoring, wear, and seizure marks.
- Pistons checked for cracks, carbon buildup, and skirt wear.
- Piston rings checked for wear and damage.
- Crankshaft journals checked for scoring and wear.
- Bearings checked for scratches, copper exposure, and abnormal wear.
- Timing chain, guides, and tensioner checked for wear.

## 8. Engine Assembly Procedure

Before assembly, all parts were cleaned and inspected. Bearing surfaces, cylinder walls, piston rings, crankshaft journals, and bolt threads were lubricated with clean engine oil where required.

### 8.1 Block Preparation

The cylinder block was cleaned and checked before installation of internal components. Oil passages and coolant passages were checked for dirt or blockage. Old gasket material was removed from sealing surfaces.

Preparation steps:

- Clean cylinder block surface.
- Clean bearing saddles and caps.
- Clean crankshaft journals.
- Apply engine oil to bearing surfaces.
- Install correct main bearings in their original positions.
- Install thrust bearings correctly.

### 8.2 Crankshaft Installation

The crankshaft was installed into the cylinder block after the main bearings were fitted. Main bearing caps were installed in the correct order and direction.

Important points:

- Main bearing caps must be installed in their original positions.
- Bearing surfaces must be lubricated with clean engine oil.
- Main bearing cap bolts should be tightened gradually.
- The crankshaft must rotate smoothly by hand after tightening.
- Crankshaft end play must be checked after installation.

### 8.3 Piston and Connecting Rod Installation

Piston rings were installed on the pistons. The ring gaps were positioned correctly around the piston and not aligned together. A piston ring compressor was used to install the piston and connecting rod assemblies into the cylinders.

Important points:

- Pistons and rods must be installed into their correct cylinders.
- The front mark on the piston must face the front of the engine.
- Connecting rod bolts must not scratch the crankshaft journals.
- Piston rings and cylinder walls must be lubricated.
- Connecting rod caps must match their original rods.

### 8.4 Cylinder Head Installation

A new cylinder head gasket was installed before fitting the cylinder head. The cylinder head bolts were tightened using the manufacturer procedure.

For the QG15DE, the cylinder head bolt procedure is:

| Step | Procedure |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tighten bolts to 29 Nm |
| 2 | Tighten bolts to 59 Nm |
| 3 | Loosen bolts completely |
| 4 | Tighten bolts again to 29 Nm |
| 5 | Turn bolts 50 - 55 degrees clockwise |
| 6 | Tighten small front bolts to 6.3 - 8.3 Nm |

Important note: Cylinder head bolts require angular tightening. Bolt threads and seat surfaces should be clean and coated with engine oil.

### 8.5 Connecting Rod Cap Nut Tightening

For the QG15DE, the connecting rod cap nut procedure is:

| Step | Procedure |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tighten to 13.72 - 15.68 Nm |
| 2 | Turn 35 - 40 degrees clockwise using an angle wrench |

Alternative if an angle wrench is not available:

| Alternative Procedure |
|---|
| Tighten to 23 - 28 Nm |

### 8.6 Timing Alignment

The timing chain was installed by aligning the timing marks on the crankshaft sprocket, camshaft sprockets, and timing chain. The chain tensioner and guides were installed correctly.

Important points:

- No. 1 piston must be at TDC.
- Timing marks must align correctly.
- Camshaft and crankshaft should not be rotated separately before timing is installed.
- Chain tensioner must be correctly released after installation.
- Engine should be rotated by hand to confirm there is no interference.

## 9. Final Checks and Testing

After assembly, final checks were completed before considering the engine ready.

The following checks were carried out:

- Confirmed all bolts and nuts were tightened.
- Confirmed the crankshaft rotated freely by hand.
- Checked timing marks after rotating the engine.
- Checked that pistons and valves did not contact.
- Checked that all gaskets and seals were correctly installed.
- Checked that oil pan and covers were installed properly.
- Checked that external components were refitted correctly.
- Checked that electrical connectors, hoses, and fuel lines were connected.

If the engine is installed in a vehicle, further checks should include:

- Refill engine oil.
- Refill coolant.
- Check for oil and coolant leaks.
- Start the engine and listen for abnormal noise.
- Check oil pressure warning light.
- Check engine temperature.
- Carry out compression test if required.

## 12. Conclusion

In conclusion, this practical helped develop an understanding of the construction, disassembly, inspection, measurement, and assembly of the Nissan QG15DE engine. The practical showed the importance of following correct procedures, using suitable tools, keeping parts organized, and applying manufacturer specifications.

The activity also showed that engine assembly requires accuracy and cleanliness. Important components such as the crankshaft, pistons, connecting rods, bearings, cylinder head, and timing chain must be installed correctly. Measurements such as cylinder bore, piston ring gap, crankshaft end play, and bearing clearance are necessary to determine whether parts are serviceable.

The final check confirmed that the engine should rotate freely by hand and that all fasteners must be secure before the engine is started or returned to service.
