## Chapter 1 - Engine Components and Workshop Safety

Must know:

- An internal combustion engine converts chemical energy in fuel into mechanical energy.
- Combustion happens inside the cylinder.
- Petrol engines use spark ignition.
- Diesel engines use compression ignition.
- Main engine components: engine block, cylinder head, piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, camshaft, valves, spark plug or injector, timing belt/chain, cooling system, and lubrication system.

Four-stroke cycle:

1. Intake: piston moves down, inlet valve opens, air-fuel mixture or air enters.
2. Compression: piston moves up, both valves closed, charge is compressed.
3. Power: combustion pushes piston down; this is the only power-producing stroke.
4. Exhaust: piston moves up, exhaust valve opens, burnt gases leave.

Petrol vs diesel:

- Petrol: air-fuel mixture is ignited by spark plug; lower compression ratio.
- Diesel: air is compressed first, fuel is injected, heat of compression ignites fuel; higher compression ratio and better efficiency.

Workshop safety:

- Wear PPE: goggles, gloves, safety shoes, suitable clothing, hearing protection where needed.
- Keep the workshop clean, dry, ventilated, and well-lit.
- Use correct tools and check tools before use.
- Never work on a hot engine unless required and controlled.
- Secure the vehicle before working: parking brake, wheel chocks, jack stands.
- Clean spills immediately.
- Be alert for fire, hot parts, moving parts, chemicals, and electrical shock.

## Chapter 2 - Move, Position and Park Vehicle

Must know:

- Before moving a vehicle, check surroundings, seat position, mirrors, brakes, gear selector, warning lights, and customer/job instructions.
- For manual vehicles, coordinate clutch, brake, accelerator, and gear selection.
- For automatic vehicles, confirm brake is pressed before selecting gear.
- Always move slowly inside workshop areas.
- Use a spotter if visibility is poor.

Parking methods:

- Parallel parking: position beside front vehicle, reverse carefully, steer into space, straighten, keep safe distance.
- Perpendicular parking: approach slowly, align with bay, turn in, straighten wheels.
- Angle parking: signal, approach at low speed, enter along bay angle.

Safe lifting:

- Park on level ground.
- Apply parking brake and chock wheels.
- Use correct jacking points.
- Lift with a suitable jack.
- Support with jack stands before going under vehicle.
- Never rely on a jack alone.

Vehicle intake:

- Record customer name, contact, vehicle details, complaint/job request, symptoms, and preferred communication.
- Check pre-existing damage before service.
- Record basic checks such as water, oil, fan belt, and tires.
- Complete quality check before handover.

## Chapter 3 - Wash and Clean Vehicle Body and Interior

Must know:

- Use a systematic process: interior first, then exterior.
- Work top to bottom.
- Wash in shade on a cool surface to avoid soap drying on paint.
- Use separate wheel tools because brake dust can scratch paint.

Exterior cleaning process:

1. Clean wheels and tires first.
2. Pre-rinse vehicle from top to bottom.
3. Use two-bucket method: one soap bucket, one rinse bucket.
4. Use pH-neutral car shampoo and microfiber wash mitt.
5. Wash in straight lines, not circles.
6. Rinse thoroughly.
7. Dry with microfiber towel; do not air dry.
8. Clean glass with auto glass cleaner.
9. Apply wax or sealant where required.

Interior cleaning:

- Remove rubbish and personal items carefully.
- Vacuum seats, carpet, mats, and boot.
- Clean dashboard, vents, panels, and glass.
- Use correct product for fabric, vinyl, plastic, or leather.

## Chapter 4 - Inspect and Service Engine

Engine inspection means checking the engine for wear, leaks, damage, abnormal noises, overheating, poor performance, or fault codes.

Inspection areas:

- Visual checks: cracks, leaks, corrosion, loose parts, broken mounts, damaged belts/hoses.
- Fluid checks: engine oil, coolant, brake fluid, transmission fluid, power steering fluid where applicable.
- Oil quality: check level, color, smell, contamination, and service interval.
- Coolant: check level, leaks, hose condition, radiator cap, overheating signs.
- Diagnostic testing: use OBD scanner to read diagnostic trouble codes.
- Performance checks: listen for knocking, squealing, rattling, misfire, rough idle, smoke, overheating, or loss of power.

Service actions:

- Change engine oil and oil filter.
- Replace air filter and fuel filter when required.
- Check/replace spark plugs.
- Check belts, hoses, coolant, and battery terminals.
- Clear codes only after diagnosis and repair.

## Chapter 5 and 15 - Auto Air-Conditioning System

The A/C system cools, dehumidifies, and circulates cabin air. It moves heat from inside the vehicle to outside using refrigerant.

Main components:

- Compressor: compresses low-pressure, low-temperature gas into high-pressure, high-temperature gas.
- Condenser: removes heat and changes refrigerant into high-pressure liquid.
- Receiver/dryer or accumulator: stores refrigerant and removes moisture/debris.
- Expansion valve or orifice tube: drops pressure and controls refrigerant flow into evaporator.
- Evaporator: absorbs heat from cabin air and evaporates refrigerant.
- Blower fan: moves cooled air into cabin.

Cycle order:

Compressor -> condenser -> receiver/dryer or accumulator -> expansion valve/orifice tube -> evaporator -> compressor.

Common faults:

- Warm/no cold air: low refrigerant, leak, faulty compressor, blocked condenser.
- Weak airflow: clogged cabin filter, weak blower motor, blocked vents, evaporator problem.
- Noise: compressor bearing, clutch, loose belt, debris in blower.
- Smell: mold/bacteria in evaporator or drain problem.
- Electrical issue: fuse, relay, pressure switch, clutch coil, wiring, control panel.

Leak detection methods:

- Visual inspection for oily residue.
- UV dye and lamp.
- Electronic leak detector.
- Soapy water bubble test.
- Nitrogen pressure test.

## Chapter 6 - Petrol Fuel System

Purpose: store, filter, and deliver petrol to the engine in the correct amount for combustion.

Main components:

- Fuel tank: stores petrol.
- Fuel cap and filler neck: allow refueling and prevent leakage.
- Fuel pump: sends fuel from tank to engine under pressure.
- Fuel filter: removes dirt and contaminants.
- Fuel lines: carry fuel.
- Fuel injectors: spray fuel into intake port or cylinder.
- Carburetor: older system that mixes fuel and air mechanically.

Fuel delivery types:

- Carburetor: older mechanical mixing system.
- Single-point injection: one injector supplies intake manifold.
- Multi-point injection: one injector per cylinder near intake valve.
- Direct injection: injector sprays directly into combustion chamber.

Clogged fuel filter symptoms:

- Hard starting.
- Loss of power.
- Engine hesitation.
- Misfire.
- Stalling.
- Poor acceleration.

## Chapter 7 - Diesel Fuel System

Diesel engines ignite fuel by compression heat, not spark plugs.

Main systems:

- Fuel tank.
- Lift pump/transfer pump.
- Fuel filter and water separator.
- High-pressure fuel pump.
- Injector nozzles.
- ECU/ECM control on modern systems.

Important components:

- Lift pump: moves fuel from tank to high-pressure pump.
- Diesel fuel filter: removes dirt and water to protect injectors and pump.
- High-pressure fuel pump: raises fuel pressure for injection.
- Injector: atomizes fuel into combustion chamber at the correct time.
- ECU: receives sensor inputs, processes data, controls actuators and injection.

High-pressure diesel systems:

- Common rail: high-pressure rail feeds electronically controlled injectors.
- EUI: each cylinder has injector and pump unit.
- HEUI: hydraulic actuation with electronic control.
- Distributor pump: one pump distributes fuel to cylinders.
- Inline pump: one pumping element per cylinder in a line.

Diesel service focus:

- Keep fuel clean and water-free.
- Replace filters on schedule.
- Bleed air after fuel system service where required.
- Do not contaminate high-pressure components.

## Chapter 8 - Ignition System

Purpose: create a high-voltage spark to ignite the air-fuel mixture in petrol engines.

Primary and secondary circuits:

- Primary circuit: low voltage from battery through ignition switch and coil primary winding.
- Secondary circuit: high voltage from coil to spark plug.

Main components:

- Battery: supplies low-voltage power.
- Ignition switch: controls circuit.
- Ignition coil: steps 12 V up to high voltage.
- Spark plug: creates spark in combustion chamber.
- Distributor: older system that sends high voltage to each plug.
- ECU/ignition module: controls timing in modern systems.

Spark plug types:

- Copper.
- Platinum.
- Iridium.

Ignition system types:

- Conventional breaker-point ignition.
- Electronic ignition.
- Distributorless ignition system.
- Coil-on-plug system.

Common faults:

- Hard starting.
- Misfire.
- Rough idle.
- Poor acceleration.
- High fuel consumption.
- No spark.
- Damaged plugs, coils, leads, distributor, or sensors.

## Chapter 9 - Steering System

Purpose: convert driver steering wheel movement into controlled wheel movement.

Functions:

- Directional control.
- Stability.
- Road feedback.
- Driver comfort and control.

Main components:

- Steering wheel.
- Steering column and shaft.
- Universal joints.
- Steering gear.
- Rack and pinion.
- Recirculating ball steering gear.
- Linkages, tie rods, steering arms.
- Steering knuckle.
- Power steering pump/motor.

Steering gear types:

- Rack and pinion: common in modern cars; direct and precise.
- Recirculating ball: common in older/larger vehicles; durable.

Power steering:

- Hydraulic power steering: engine-driven pump creates hydraulic pressure.
- Electric power steering: electric motor provides assist only when needed.
- Four-wheel steering: rear wheels also steer for maneuverability and stability.

Inspection points:

- Free play at steering wheel.
- Fluid level/leaks for hydraulic systems.
- Noise when turning.
- Tie rod end wear.
- Ball joint and bushing condition.
- Steering rack boots.
- Uneven tire wear and alignment symptoms.

## Chapter 10A and 10B - Transmission System and Manual Transmission

Purpose: transfer engine power to drive wheels and provide suitable torque and speed.

Why transmission is needed:

- Internal combustion engines work best in a limited RPM range.
- Low gear gives high torque and low speed.
- High gear gives lower torque and higher speed.

Transmission types:

- Manual transmission.
- Automatic transmission.
- CVT.
- Sequential transmission.
- DCT.
- AMT.

Manual transmission main parts:

- Clutch: connects/disconnects engine from transmission.
- Gearbox: houses shafts, gears, bearings, and shift mechanism.
- Input shaft: receives engine power.
- Output shaft: sends power to final drive.
- Gears: provide ratios.
- Shift mechanism: selects gear.

Manual shifting process:

1. Press clutch pedal.
2. Move gear lever to required gear.
3. Release clutch smoothly while applying throttle.

Gear ratio:

- Gear ratio = driven gear teeth / driving gear teeth.
- Higher numerical ratio gives more torque and lower speed.
- Lower numerical ratio gives less torque and higher speed.

## Chapter 11 - Automatic Transmission

An automatic transmission changes gear ratios automatically without a clutch pedal.

Main components:

- Torque converter.
- Transmission pump.
- Planetary gearsets.
- Hydraulic system.
- Valve body.
- ECU/TCU.
- ATF.

Torque converter:

- Replaces manual clutch.
- Uses fluid coupling.
- Main parts: impeller/pump, turbine, stator.
- Allows smooth take-off and torque multiplication.

Transmission pump:

- Generates hydraulic pressure.
- Circulates ATF.
- Lubricates and cools transmission parts.

Planetary gearset:

- Sun gear.
- Planet gears.
- Ring gear.
- Planet carrier.
- Provides different gear ratios in compact space.

Valve body:

- Hydraulic control center.
- Contains passages, valves, springs, and solenoids.
- Directs ATF to clutches and bands for gear shifts.

TCU:

- Controls shift timing and quality.
- Uses inputs such as speed, throttle position, load, and temperature.

ATF functions:

- Hydraulic fluid for gear changes.
- Power transfer medium in torque converter.
- Lubricant.
- Coolant.
- Friction control for clutches.

## Chapter 12 - Brake System

Purpose: slow or stop vehicle by converting kinetic energy into heat through friction.

Main types:

- Disc brake.
- Drum brake.
- ABS.
- Regenerative braking.
- Parking brake/handbrake.

Main components:

- Brake pedal.
- Master cylinder.
- Brake booster/servo.
- Brake fluid.
- Brake lines/hoses.
- Caliper.
- Brake pads.
- Disc/rotor.
- Brake drum.
- Wheel cylinder.
- Parking brake cable/switch/motor.

Hydraulic brake operation:

1. Driver presses brake pedal.
2. Master cylinder creates hydraulic pressure.
3. Pressure travels through brake fluid.
4. Caliper or wheel cylinder applies friction material.
5. Friction slows the wheel.

ABS:

- Wheel speed sensors monitor wheel speed.
- ECU detects wheel lock.
- Hydraulic modulator rapidly reduces and reapplies pressure.
- Helps maintain steering control during hard braking.

Common brake faults:

- Squealing: worn pads or pad vibration.
- Grinding: metal-to-metal contact.
- Soft pedal: air in system, leak, low fluid, master cylinder fault.
- Pulling to one side: uneven braking, stuck caliper, contaminated pad, tire issue.
- Vibration: warped disc/rotor or uneven surface.
- ABS warning light: sensor, wiring, ring, or module fault.

## Chapter 13 - Auto Electric System

Purpose: generate, store, distribute, and control electrical power.

Main components:

- Battery.
- Alternator/charging system.
- Starter motor.
- Starter solenoid.
- Ignition switch/start button.
- Fuses and relays.
- Wiring and connectors.
- ECU/ECM.
- Sensors and actuators.
- Dashboard warning lights.

Starting system:

- Battery supplies high current.
- Ignition switch/start button activates control circuit.
- Solenoid acts as high-current relay and mechanical actuator.
- Starter pinion engages flywheel.
- Starter motor cranks engine around 200-300 RPM.

Safety switches:

- Neutral safety switch on automatic transmission.
- Clutch switch on manual transmission.
- Prevent starting while in gear.

Common starting faults:

- Clicking: weak battery or loose/corroded terminals.
- Single loud click: faulty solenoid or jammed starter.
- No crank: battery, fuse, relay, switch, wiring, starter, or safety switch.
- Slow crank: weak battery, poor connection, worn starter, engine mechanical resistance.

OBD:

- OBD gives access to vehicle self-diagnostic information.
- OBD-II port is usually under dashboard on driver side.
- Standard connector has 16 pins.
- DTC example: P0300.
- First letter identifies system: P = powertrain, B = body, C = chassis, U = network.

## Chapter 14 - Final Drive Assembly Components

Purpose: transmit power from transmission to drive wheels.

Drivetrain components:

- Driveshaft/propeller shaft.
- Differential.
- Final drive gears.
- Axles/half-shafts.
- CV joints.
- Wheel hubs.

Differential:

- Allows left and right wheels to rotate at different speeds when turning.
- Transmits torque to drive wheels.

Drivetrain layouts:

- RWD: rear wheels drive vehicle; good balance and handling but weaker traction in slippery conditions.
- FWD: front wheels steer and drive; compact, efficient, common in passenger cars.
- 4WD: power to all four wheels, usually selectable, strong for off-road/low traction.
- AWD: automatically sends power to wheels as needed, good for on-road traction.

4WD modes:

- 2WD: normal dry road use.
- 4H: slippery surfaces at normal speed.
- 4L: low speed, high torque off-road use.

## Chapter 16 - Suspension System

Purpose:

- Support vehicle weight.
- Absorb road shocks.
- Keep tires in contact with road.
- Improve ride comfort, handling, braking, and stability.

Main components:

- Springs.
- Shock absorbers/dampers.
- Struts.
- Control arms/wishbones.
- Sway bars/anti-roll bars.
- Ball joints.
- Bushings.

Spring types:

- Coil spring: common passenger vehicles.
- Leaf spring: trucks, buses, heavy-duty vehicles.
- Torsion bar: twists to provide spring action.
- Air spring: uses compressed air, adjustable ride height/load support.

Shock absorber:

- Controls spring movement.
- Converts movement energy into heat.
- Prevents continuous bouncing.

Strut:

- Combines spring and shock absorber in a structural unit.

Suspension types:

- Dependent: wheels connected by axle.
- Semi-independent: compromise between dependent and independent.
- Independent: each wheel moves separately.
- MacPherson strut: common independent front suspension.
- Double wishbone: two control arms; good handling and geometry control.
- Multi-link: multiple links for balance of comfort and control.
- Air suspension: adjustable height and comfort.

Inspection points:

- Vehicle sits level.
- Bounce test for weak dampers.
- Leaks from shocks/struts.
- Broken springs.
- Worn bushings or ball joints.
- Noise over bumps.
- Uneven tire wear.
- Poor handling or pulling.
