## 1. Internal Combustion Engine

### Definition

An internal combustion engine converts chemical energy in fuel into mechanical
work. Combustion happens inside the cylinder.

### Main Types

- **Petrol engine:** Uses spark ignition.
- **Diesel engine:** Uses compression ignition.

### Basic Working Principle

Fuel burns inside the cylinder. The expanding gas pushes the piston down. The
connecting rod transfers this movement to the crankshaft. The crankshaft changes
the piston's up-and-down motion into rotary motion.

## 2. Four-Stroke Cycle

### Four Strokes in Order

```text
Intake -> Compression -> Power -> Exhaust
```

### Simple Diagram

```text
1. Intake       Piston moves down, inlet valve opens
2. Compression  Piston moves up, both valves closed
3. Power        Combustion pushes piston down
4. Exhaust      Piston moves up, exhaust valve opens
```

### Intake Stroke

- Piston moves downward.
- Inlet valve opens.
- Exhaust valve closes.
- Air-fuel mixture enters a petrol engine.
- Air enters a diesel engine.

### Compression Stroke

- Both valves are closed.
- Piston moves upward.
- The mixture or air is compressed.
- Pressure and temperature increase.
- Petrol engine: spark plug ignites the mixture.
- Diesel engine: fuel is injected and ignites from compression heat.

### Power Stroke

- Combustion occurs.
- High-pressure gases push the piston downward.
- This is the only stroke that produces power.
- The crankshaft converts this movement into rotational energy.

### Exhaust Stroke

- Exhaust valve opens.
- Inlet valve closes.
- Piston moves upward.
- Burnt gases are pushed out of the cylinder.

## 3. Petrol and Diesel Engines

### Petrol Engine

- Uses petrol/gasoline fuel.
- Uses spark ignition.
- Air-fuel mixture is ignited by a spark plug.
- Lower compression ratio than diesel engines.
- Smooth and quiet operation.
- Common in cars, motorcycles, scooters, small trucks, generators, small
  machinery, light aircraft, small boats, and jet skis.

### Diesel Engine

- Uses diesel fuel.
- Uses compression ignition.
- Air is compressed first, then diesel fuel is injected.
- Heat from compression ignites the fuel.
- Higher compression ratio than petrol engines.
- Better fuel economy and higher torque.
- Common in trucks, buses, tractors, generators, marine vessels, industrial
  equipment, and locomotives.

### Petrol vs Diesel Table

| Feature | Petrol Engine | Diesel Engine |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel | Petrol/gasoline | Diesel |
| Ignition | Spark ignition | Compression ignition |
| Compression ratio | Lower | Higher |
| Operation | Smooth and quiet | Strong torque |
| Common use | Light vehicles | Heavy-duty vehicles |

## 4. Engine Main Components

### Component Diagram

```text
Cylinder Head
   |-- Valves
   |-- Spark plug / injector
   |-- Camshaft
   |
Engine Block
   |-- Cylinders
   |-- Pistons
   |-- Connecting rods
   |-- Crankshaft
   |
Support Systems
   |-- Timing belt / chain
   |-- Oil pump
   |-- Water pump
   |-- Radiator
   |-- Fuel injector / carburetor
   |-- Flywheel / flexplate
```

### Components and Functions

- **Engine block:** Main structure of the engine. Houses cylinders and oil/coolant
  passages.
- **Cylinder:** Space where the piston moves up and down.
- **Piston:** Converts expanding gas energy into mechanical motion.
- **Connecting rod:** Connects piston to crankshaft.
- **Crankshaft:** Converts piston linear motion into rotary motion.
- **Cylinder head:** Seals the cylinders and holds valves, spark plugs, and
  camshaft in overhead-cam engines.
- **Valves:** Control intake and exhaust flow.
- **Camshaft:** Opens and closes the valves.
- **Timing belt/chain:** Synchronizes crankshaft and camshaft rotation.
- **Spark plug:** Ignites the air-fuel mixture in petrol engines.
- **Fuel injector/carburetor:** Delivers fuel to the engine.
- **Oil pump:** Circulates engine oil to lubricate moving parts.
- **Water pump:** Circulates coolant through the engine and radiator.
- **Radiator:** Cools engine coolant and helps prevent overheating.
- **Flywheel/flexplate:** Stores rotational energy and helps smooth engine
  rotation.

## 5. Engine Timing

### Meaning of Engine Timing

Engine timing is the precise synchronization between the crankshaft and camshaft.
It makes sure the valves open and close at the correct time compared with piston
position.

### Timing Belt or Timing Chain Function

The timing belt or chain synchronizes the crankshaft and camshaft so that valve
operation happens correctly.

### Why Timing Marks Matter

Timing marks help align the crankshaft and camshaft correctly. If timing is
wrong, the engine may run badly or become damaged.

### Why No. 1 Piston Is Set at TDC

No. 1 piston is set at TDC on the compression stroke before timing-chain work so
the correct timing reference is maintained.

## 6. Compression Pressure Test

### Purpose

A compression test checks cylinder sealing and engine condition. Low compression
can indicate worn piston rings, damaged valves, blown head gasket, worn cylinder
walls, or incorrect timing.

### Basic Procedure from Course Material

1. Warm up the engine.
2. Turn ignition switch off.
3. Release fuel pressure.
4. Remove ignition coils.
5. Remove spark plugs.
6. Clean around the spark plug area with compressed air.
7. Attach compression tester to No. 1 cylinder.
8. Depress accelerator pedal fully to keep throttle valve open.
9. Crank the engine and record the highest gauge reading.
10. Repeat the measurement for each cylinder.

### QG15DE Compression Figures Shown in Lecturer Material

- **Standard:** 1,372 kPa / 198.9 psi at 350 RPM.
- **Minimum:** 1,176 kPa / 170.5 psi at 350 RPM.
- **Maximum difference between cylinders:** 98 kPa / 14 psi at 350 RPM.

## 7. Valve Clearance

### Purpose

Valve clearance allows correct valve operation when the engine is running and
parts expand with heat.

### Checking Method from Course Material

1. Check valve clearance while the engine is warm and not running.
2. Remove rocker cover.
3. Remove all spark plugs.
4. Set No. 1 cylinder at TDC on compression stroke.
5. Align the pointer with the TDC mark on the crankshaft pulley.
6. Use a feeler gauge between the valve lifter and camshaft.
7. Record any clearance measurements that are out of specification.

## 8. Common Engine Faults

### Starting Faults

| Fault | Possible Cause | Rectification |
|---|---|---|
| Engine fails to start | Weak or dead battery | Recharge or replace battery |
| Hard starting | Faulty spark plug or ignition coil | Clean or replace spark plug |
| Poor compression | Worn piston rings or valves | Replace worn parts |
| Fuel not reaching engine | Blocked fuel line or filter | Clean fuel system |

Other causes from the course material:

- Corroded battery terminals.
- Bad starter motor.
- Faulty ignition switch.
- Park/neutral safety switch failure.
- No fuel.
- Bad fuel pump or relay.
- No spark.
- Broken timing belt or chain.

## 9. Poor Compression

### Symptoms

- Difficulty starting.
- Rough idle.
- Reduced power.
- Misfire.
- Increased oil consumption.
- Increased fuel consumption.
- Abnormal exhaust smoke.

### Common Causes

- Worn piston rings.
- Damaged valves.
- Blown head gasket.
- Worn cylinder walls.
- Improper timing.

## 10. Poor Engine Performance

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Rectification |
|---|---|---|
| Lack of power | Clogged air filter or fuel injector | Clean or replace |
| Engine misfires | Faulty spark plug, plug wire, or injector | Inspect and replace |
| High fuel consumption | Incorrect carburetor/injection setting | Tune fuel system |
| Irregular idling | Vacuum leak or improper timing | Adjust timing and check hoses |

## 11. Engine Overheating

### Causes and Rectification

- **Low coolant or leaking radiator:** Refill coolant and repair leak.
- **Faulty thermostat:** Replace thermostat.
- **Blocked radiator fins:** Clean radiator.
- **Water pump failure:** Replace defective parts.

## 12. Lubrication System Faults

| Fault | Cause | Rectification |
|---|---|---|
| Low oil pressure | Insufficient oil or clogged filter | Add oil or replace filter |
| Excessive oil consumption | Worn piston rings or valve seals | Replace worn parts |
| Engine knocking | Wrong oil grade or low oil | Use correct grade and refill |
| Oil leakage | Damaged gasket or seal | Replace gasket or seal |

## 13. Combustion and Firing Faults

| Fault | Cause | Rectification |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-ignition / knocking | Low octane fuel or carbon deposits | Use correct fuel and clean chamber |
| Backfiring | Incorrect ignition timing | Adjust timing |
| Detonation | Overheating or high compression | Cool engine and check compression |
| Smoke emission | Faulty injector or carburetor setting | Adjust or replace |

## 14. Exhaust Smoke

| Smoke Color | Possible Cause | Rectification |
|---|---|---|
| Black smoke | Excess fuel / rich mixture | Adjust air-fuel ratio |
| Blue smoke | Oil burning from worn rings or valve seals | Replace worn components |
| White smoke | Coolant leakage into combustion chamber | Check head gasket and repair leak |

## 15. Mechanical Noise and Vibration

| Symptom | Cause | Rectification |
|---|---|---|
| Knocking sound | Loose connecting rod or worn bearing | Tighten or replace parts |
| Tapping noise | Incorrect valve clearance | Adjust clearance |
| Vibration | Unbalanced crankshaft or loose mounts | Balance and tighten |
| Timing-chain noise | Slack or worn chain | Replace or adjust tensioner |

## 16. Diagnostic Tools

Diagnostic tools listed in the course material:

- Compression tester.
- Vacuum gauge.
- Multimeter.
- Engine scanner / OBD-II scanner.
- Stethoscope.

## Quick Revision List

- Four strokes: intake, compression, power, exhaust.
- Petrol uses spark ignition.
- Diesel uses compression ignition.
- Crankshaft changes up-and-down piston motion into rotary motion.
- Timing chain synchronizes crankshaft and camshaft.
- Power stroke is the only power-producing stroke.
- Black smoke means excess fuel.
- Blue smoke means oil burning.
- White smoke means coolant leakage into combustion chamber.
- Overheating can be caused by low coolant, radiator leak, thermostat fault,
  blocked radiator, or water-pump failure.
