## AEP01 - Automotive Engine Practice

### Petrol and Diesel Engines

Internal combustion engines convert chemical energy in fuel into mechanical work.

Petrol engine:

- Uses spark ignition.
- Air-fuel mixture is ignited by spark plug.
- Lower compression ratio, commonly around 6:1 to 10:1.
- Smooth and quiet.
- Common in cars, motorcycles, small trucks, generators, and light machinery.

Diesel engine:

- Uses compression ignition.
- Air is compressed until hot, then diesel is injected.
- Higher compression ratio.
- Better torque and fuel economy.
- Common in buses, trucks, tractors, generators, marine and heavy equipment.

### Four-Stroke Cycle

1. Intake: piston moves down, inlet valve opens, mixture or air enters.
2. Compression: piston moves up, both valves closed, charge is compressed.
3. Power: combustion pushes piston down; this is the power-producing stroke.
4. Exhaust: piston moves up, exhaust valve opens, burnt gas exits.

### Main Engine Components and Functions

- Engine block: main body; houses cylinders and crankcase.
- Cylinder: bore where piston moves.
- Piston: converts gas pressure into movement.
- Connecting rod: connects piston to crankshaft.
- Crankshaft: converts piston up/down motion into rotary motion.
- Cylinder head: seals cylinders and holds valves, spark plugs/injectors, and camshaft.
- Valves: control intake and exhaust flow.
- Camshaft: opens and closes valves.
- Timing belt/chain: synchronizes camshaft and crankshaft.
- Spark plug: ignites petrol air-fuel mixture.
- Fuel injector/carburetor: supplies fuel.
- Oil pump: circulates oil for lubrication.
- Water pump: circulates coolant.
- Radiator: removes heat from coolant.
- Flywheel/flexplate: stores rotational energy and smooths engine rotation.

### Engine Disassembly and Assembly

Before disassembly:

- Prepare clean workspace.
- Secure engine on stand.
- Drain oil and coolant safely.
- Arrange tools and trays.
- Label parts, bolts, caps, rods, and bearings.
- Remove external components such as intake, exhaust, alternator, belts, hoses, connectors, coils, and spark plugs.

Cylinder head removal:

1. Remove valve cover.
2. Set No. 1 piston at TDC on compression stroke.
3. Record timing marks.
4. Remove timing chain components carefully.
5. Loosen head bolts gradually in correct sequence.
6. Lift cylinder head and place on clean surface.

Bottom end teardown:

1. Remove oil pan.
2. Remove oil strainer/oil pump if required.
3. Remove connecting rod caps.
4. Push piston and rod assemblies out carefully.
5. Keep piston/rod assemblies in cylinder order.
6. Remove main bearing caps.
7. Remove crankshaft, bearings, and thrust bearings.

Assembly rules:

- Clean all parts and oil passages.
- Use correct bearing positions.
- Lubricate bearings, journals, rings, cylinder walls, and bolt threads where specified.
- Keep piston ring gaps correctly spaced.
- Use piston ring compressor.
- Tighten bolts gradually and to specification.
- Align timing marks before final rotation.
- Rotate engine by hand after assembly to confirm free movement.

### QG15DE Practical Facts

The AEP01 assignment engine is Nissan QG15DE.

Key specs:

- Petrol/gasoline engine.
- 4-cylinder in-line.
- DOHC.
- Displacement: 1,497 cm3.
- Bore x stroke: 73.6 mm x 88.0 mm.
- Firing order: 1-3-4-2.
- Compression ratio: 9.9:1.
- Spark plug: NGK BKR5E-11.
- Spark plug gap: 1.0-1.1 mm.
- Standard compression pressure: 1,372 kPa / 198.9 psi at 350 rpm.
- Minimum compression pressure: 1,176 kPa / 170.5 psi.
- Maximum cylinder difference: 98 kPa / 14 psi.

Important QG15DE checks:

- Cylinder head flatness.
- Cylinder bore wear, taper, and out-of-round.
- Piston-to-bore clearance.
- Piston ring end gap.
- Crankshaft end play.
- Main bearing clearance.
- Connecting rod bearing clearance.
- Connecting rod side clearance.

### Common Engine Faults

Starting faults:

- Weak/dead battery: recharge or replace.
- Bad starter motor: test and replace if faulty.
- Park/neutral switch fault: inspect and repair.
- No spark: check spark plugs, coils, ignition wiring, sensors.
- Fuel not reaching engine: check tank level, fuel pump, filter, lines, carburetor/injector.
- Poor compression: test compression; inspect rings, valves, head gasket, cylinder wear.

Performance faults:

- Lack of power: clogged air filter, fuel restriction, injector problem, low compression.
- Misfire: spark plug, coil, fuel injector, compression, vacuum leak.
- Rough idle: air/fuel issue, ignition issue, vacuum leak, dirty throttle body.

Overheating faults:

- Low coolant.
- Leaking radiator/hose.
- Faulty thermostat.
- Faulty water pump.
- Blocked radiator.
- Cooling fan fault.

Lubrication faults:

- Low oil pressure: low oil, clogged filter, worn pump, worn bearings.
- Excessive oil consumption: worn rings, valve seals, leaks.

Exhaust smoke:

- Black smoke: rich mixture or excess fuel.
- Blue smoke: oil burning.
- White smoke: coolant entering combustion chamber or condensation.

Diagnostic tools:

- Compression tester.
- Vacuum gauge.
- Multimeter.
- OBD-II scanner.
- Stethoscope.
- Feeler gauge, micrometer, bore gauge, dial gauge.

## BWP01 - Basic Workshop Practice

### Workshop Safety

Workshop safety means rules and procedures that prevent injury and damage.

Main safety rules:

- Wear PPE.
- Use correct tools.
- Keep tools maintained.
- Keep floor clean and dry.
- Clean oil spills immediately.
- Maintain ventilation.
- Keep fire hazards controlled.
- Store sharp tools safely.
- Do not rush.
- Follow teacher/supervisor instructions.

PPE types:

- Head protection.
- Eye and face protection.
- Hearing protection.
- Hand protection.
- Respiratory protection.
- Foot protection.
- Fall protection where required.

### Manual Lifting

Manual lifting means lifting, carrying, pushing, or pulling without a machine.

Correct lifting method:

1. Stand close to the object.
2. Keep feet stable.
3. Bend knees, not back.
4. Keep back straight.
5. Grip firmly with both hands.
6. Lift smoothly using leg muscles.
7. Keep load close to body.
8. Do not twist while lifting.

Possible injuries:

- Muscle strain/sprain.
- Herniated disc.
- Hernia.
- Tendinitis/bursitis.
- Stress injuries.

- Hacksaw: cuts metal/plastic rods and pipes.

- Screwdrivers: flathead, Phillips, Torx.
- Spanners/wrenches: open, ring, combination, adjustable.
- Socket wrench: turns nuts/bolts with sockets.
- Allen keys: hex socket screws.

Gripping tools:

- Pliers.
- Needle-nose pliers.
- Combination pliers.
- Locking pliers.

Striking tools:

- Hammer.

Holding tools:

Shaping/finishing tools:

### Measuring and Marking Tools

Common tools:

- Tape measure.
- Steel rule.
- Try square.
- Combination square.
- Marking gauge.
- Scriber.
- Pencil/marker.
- Vernier caliper.
- Micrometer.

Exam focus:

- Measuring tools must be clean and zeroed.
- Read at eye level to avoid parallax error.
- Mark clearly before cutting/drilling.
- Check dimensions twice before machining.

### Bench Vice, Files, Hacksaw

Bench vice:

- Holds work securely.
- Use soft jaws for delicate work.
- Do not overtighten and damage the workpiece.

Files:

- Used for smoothing and shaping.
- Use handle fitted securely.
- Push file forward; reduce pressure on return.
- Clean file teeth with file card.

Hacksaw:

- Used for cutting metal/plastic.
- Teeth should face forward.
- Use steady strokes.
- Keep blade tension correct.
- Clamp work securely before cutting.

### Chisels and Hammers

Chisels:

- Used for cutting, shaping, or removing material.
- Keep cutting edge sharp.
- Do not use mushroomed chisel heads.

Hammers:

- Select correct hammer for task.
- Check handle is secure.
- Strike squarely.
- Wear eye protection.

### Drills, Taps, Countersinking and Dies

Drilling machine parts:

- Bed.
- Pillar.
- Swivel table.
- Motor.
- Stepped pulley.
- Spindle.
- Chuck.
- Drill bit.
- Hand wheel.

Drilling safety:

- Clamp workpiece.
- Wear eye protection.
- Remove chuck key before starting.
- Use correct speed.
- Use cutting fluid for metal.
- Keep hands away from rotating drill.
- Do not wear loose clothing.

For mild steel with HSS drill:

- Use slower speeds, often around 300-1000 RPM depending on drill diameter.
- Larger drill bits require lower RPM.
- Use cutting oil to reduce heat.

Countersinking:

- After drilling a pilot hole, a countersink bit creates a conical opening.
- Allows countersunk screw head to sit flush.

Taps and dies:

- Tap cuts internal thread in a hole.
- Die cuts external thread on a rod/bolt.
- Use correct drill size before tapping.
- Use cutting oil.
- Turn forward, then slightly back to break chips.
