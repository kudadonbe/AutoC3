# BWP01 Exam Topics Answers

These answers are for the listed exam topics and are based on the BWP01 course
material in `modules/BWP01-basic-workshop-practice/learning-materials/`.

## 1. Drill Machine

### Parts and Functions

- **Bed:** Supports the whole machine.
- **Pillar:** Vertical support column for the machine.
- **Swivel table:** Holds the workpiece and can be adjusted for position.
- **Motor:** Provides power to rotate the drill.
- **Stepped pulley:** Changes drilling speed.
- **Spindle:** Rotating shaft that carries the chuck.
- **Chuck:** Holds the drill bit tightly.
- **Drill bit:** Cutting tool that makes the hole.
- **Hand wheel:** Moves the spindle and drill bit up and down.

### Safety Points

- Clamp the workpiece.
- Remove the chuck key before starting.
- Wear eye protection.
- Use the correct speed.
- Use cutting oil for metal.
- Keep hands away from rotating parts.

For mild steel with an HSS drill bit, use a slow speed, usually **300 to 1000
RPM** depending on drill size. Larger drill bits need lower RPM.

## 2. Vernier Caliper

### Uses

- Measuring outside diameter or thickness.
- Measuring inside diameter.
- Measuring depth.
- Measuring accurate small dimensions.

### Main Parts

- Outside jaws.
- Inside jaws.
- Main scale.
- Vernier scale.
- Depth rod.
- Locking screw or fine adjustment.

### How to Read a Vernier Caliper

1. Check zero error first.
2. Read the **main scale** just before the Vernier zero.
3. Find the Vernier line that exactly matches a main-scale line.
4. Multiply that Vernier division by the least count.
5. Add both readings.

```text
Final reading = Main scale reading + Vernier scale reading
```

Example:

```text
Main scale = 12 mm
Matching Vernier division = 5
Least count = 0.02 mm

Vernier reading = 5 x 0.02 = 0.10 mm
Final reading = 12 + 0.10 = 12.10 mm
```

## 3. Micrometer

### Uses

- Measuring small thicknesses.
- Measuring outside diameter.
- Measuring accurate small parts.

### Main Parts

- **Frame:** Holds the micrometer body.
- **Anvil:** Fixed measuring face.
- **Spindle:** Moving measuring face.
- **Measuring faces:** Contact the object being measured.
- **Sleeve with graduations:** Main scale.
- **Thimble:** Rotating scale.
- **Ratchet stop:** Applies correct measuring pressure.
- **Spindle lock:** Locks the reading.

### How to Read a Micrometer

1. Place the object between the anvil and spindle.
2. Turn the thimble using the ratchet stop.
3. Read the sleeve/main scale.
4. Read the thimble scale.
5. Add both readings.

```text
Final reading = Sleeve reading + Thimble reading
```

Example:

```text
Sleeve reading = 5.5 mm
Thimble reading = 28 x 0.01 = 0.28 mm

Final reading = 5.5 + 0.28 = 5.78 mm
```

## 4. Center Point Marking

### Purpose of Center Punching

- Marks the exact drilling point.
- Prevents the drill bit from slipping.
- Helps make the hole in the correct position.

### Correct Marking Procedure

1. Clean the workpiece.
2. Measure the required position.
3. Mark the line using a steel rule, scriber, or marking tool.
4. Check the measurement again.
5. Place the center punch or dot punch on the mark.
6. Hold the punch vertical.
7. Strike lightly with a hammer.
8. Check the mark.
9. If correct, punch deeper if needed before drilling.

## 5. Threading Tools

### Tap

A tap cuts **internal thread** inside a hole.

### Die

A die cuts **external thread** on a rod or bolt.

### Tapping Process

1. Mark and drill the correct size hole.
2. Select the correct tap.
3. Apply cutting oil.
4. Keep the tap straight.
5. Turn forward, then slightly backward to break chips.
6. Continue until the internal thread is complete.

### Dieing Process

1. Select the correct die.
2. Secure the rod or bolt.
3. Apply cutting oil.
4. Keep the die straight.
5. Turn forward, then slightly backward to break chips.
6. Continue until the external thread is complete.

### Threading Tools

- Tap.
- Die.
- Tap wrench.
- Die stock or holder.
- Drill bit.
- Cutting oil.

## 6. Workshop Tools

### Cutting Tools

- **Hacksaw:** Cuts metal or plastic pipes and rods.
- **Handsaw:** Cuts wood.
- **Utility knife:** Used for trimming, slicing, and scoring.
- **Chisel:** Cuts or shapes hard material.

### Fastening and Loosening Tools

- **Screwdriver:** Tightens or removes screws.
- **Spanner/wrench:** Turns nuts and bolts.
- **Socket wrench:** Turns nuts and bolts using sockets.
- **Allen key:** Used for hex socket screws.

### Gripping Tools

- **Pliers:** Grip, twist, and cut small parts or wires.
- **Needle-nose pliers:** Grip small parts in narrow spaces.
- **Locking pliers:** Clamp parts tightly.

### Striking Tools

- **Hammer:** Applies force.
- **Ball-peen hammer:** Used for metalwork striking.
- **Mallet:** Softer striking tool for delicate materials.

### Holding Tools

- **Bench vice:** Holds the workpiece firmly.
- **Clamp:** Temporarily holds material.
- **Tongs:** Holds hot or hazardous material.

### Measuring and Marking Tools

- **Tape measure:** Measures length or distance.
- **Steel rule:** Measures straight-line dimensions.
- **Try square:** Checks right angles.
- **Scriber/pencil:** Marks lines or drill points.

## 7. PPE

PPE means **Personal Protective Equipment**. It protects the worker from injury
in the workshop.

- **Safety shoes:** Protect feet from falling tools, sharp objects, and slipping.
- **Safety glasses:** Protect eyes from metal chips, dust, and flying particles.
- **Gloves:** Protect hands from cuts, heat, sharp edges, and dirt.
- **Ear protection:** Protects hearing from loud machines and tools.
- **Face shield:** Protects the full face from sparks, chips, and splashes.
- **Overalls:** Protect body and clothing from dirt, oil, sparks, and minor cuts.

PPE is important because it reduces injury risk and must be worn before starting
workshop work, especially drilling, cutting, grinding, hammering, or handling
sharp or hot materials.
