## BWP01 - Basic Workshop Practice

Source: `modules/BWP01-basic-workshop-practice/learning-materials/Basic Workshop Practice.pptx`

### Important Questions and Answers

1. **What is workshop safety?**

   Workshop safety is the practices, procedures, and rules designed to protect
   workers and prevent injuries in a workshop.

2. **What PPE categories are listed in the lecturer slides?**

   Head protection, eye and face protection, hearing protection, hand
   protection, respiratory protection, foot protection, and fall protection.

3. **What are the main workshop safety controls?**

   Wear PPE, maintain and correctly use tools and machines, keep the work area
   clean, provide ventilation, follow fire-safety procedures, and ensure workers
   are trained.

4. **What is manual lifting?**

   Manual lifting means lifting, carrying, pushing, or pulling an object without
   using machinery such as a forklift or crane.

5. **What is the correct lifting method?**

   Stand close to the object, bend the knees rather than the back, keep the back
   straight, grip firmly with both hands, and lift smoothly using the leg
   muscles.

6. **What injuries can result from poor manual lifting?**

   Muscle strains and sprains, herniated discs, hernias, tendinitis, bursitis,
   and stress injuries.

7. **What is a hacksaw used for?**

   It is used to cut metal or plastic pipes and rods.

8. **What are fastening and loosening tools used for?**

   They are used to drive or remove screws, nuts, and bolts. Examples include
   screwdrivers, spanners, socket wrenches, pliers, and Allen keys.

9. **What is a mallet used for?**

   It is a softer striking tool used for delicate tasks or materials.

10. **What tools are used for measuring and marking?**

    Tape measure, steel rule, try square, combination square, marking gauge,
    scriber, and pencil.

11. **What does a square check?**

    It checks right angles.

12. **What is a bench vice used for?**

    It is mounted to a workbench and holds workpieces firmly.

13. **What is a clamp used for?**

    It temporarily secures material during work such as cutting or gluing.

14. **What are files and rasps used for?**

    They are used to smooth or shape metal or wood.

15. **What is countersinking?**

    After drilling a pilot hole, a countersink bit creates a conical shape at
    the opening of the hole.

16. **What are the main drilling-machine parts listed in the slides?**

    Bed, pillar, swivel table, motor, stepped pulley, spindle, chuck, drill bit,
    and hand wheel.

17. **What drill speed is recommended for mild steel with an HSS bit?**

    The slides give a typical slow-speed range of 300 to 1000 RPM, depending on
    drill diameter. Larger drill bits require lower speeds.

18. **Why is cutting oil used during drilling?**

    It helps prevent the drill bit from overheating.

## AEP01 - Automotive Engine Practice

All source files are in

### Important Questions and Answers

1. **What does an internal combustion engine do?**

   It converts chemical energy in fuel into mechanical work.

2. **What are the four strokes in order?**

   Intake, compression, power, and exhaust.

3. **What happens during the intake stroke?**

   The piston moves downward, the inlet valve opens, and air-fuel mixture enters
   a petrol engine or air enters a diesel engine.

4. **What happens during the compression stroke?**

   The piston moves upward with both valves closed, increasing pressure and
   temperature inside the cylinder.

5. **Which stroke produces power?**

   The power or expansion stroke is the only power-producing stroke.

6. **What happens during the exhaust stroke?**

   The exhaust valve opens and the piston moves upward, pushing burnt gases out.

7. **What is the main ignition difference between petrol and diesel engines?**

   Petrol engines use spark ignition. Diesel engines use compression ignition.

8. **Where are petrol engines commonly used?**

   Cars, motorcycles, scooters, small trucks, small machinery, light aircraft,
   small boats, and jet skis.

9. **Where are diesel engines commonly used?**

   Trucks, buses, tractors, generators, ships, industrial equipment, and
   locomotives.

10. **What does the engine block do?**

    It is the engine's core structure. It houses the cylinders and contains
    passages for coolant and oil.

11. **What does a piston do?**

    It moves inside the cylinder and converts expanding-gas energy into
    mechanical motion.

12. **What does the connecting rod do?**

    It connects the piston to the crankshaft and transfers piston motion.

13. **What does the crankshaft do?**

    It converts the pistons' linear motion into rotational motion.

14. **What does the cylinder head contain?**

    It seals the top of the cylinders and contains valves, spark plugs, and
    camshafts in overhead-cam engines.

15. **What do the valves and camshaft do?**

    The valves control intake and exhaust flow. The camshaft operates the valves.

16. **What does the timing belt or timing chain do?**

    It synchronizes the crankshaft and camshaft so the valves open and close at
    the correct time.

17. **What do the oil pump, water pump, and radiator do?**

    The oil pump circulates lubricating oil. The water pump circulates coolant.
    The radiator cools the coolant and helps prevent overheating.

18. **What is engine timing?**

    It is the precise synchronization of crankshaft and camshaft movement so
    piston and valve operation occurs at the correct time.

19. **What compression-pressure figures are shown for the QG15DE?**

    Standard: 1,372 kPa or 198.9 psi at 350 RPM. Minimum: 1,176 kPa or
    170.5 psi at 350 RPM. Maximum difference between cylinders: 98 kPa or
    14 psi at 350 RPM.

20. **Why is No. 1 piston set at TDC before timing-chain work?**

    It provides the correct timing reference so the timing-chain mating marks
    can be aligned.

21. **How is valve clearance measured in the lecturer slides?**

    With the engine warm and stopped, remove the rocker cover and spark plugs,
    set No. 1 cylinder to TDC on compression, and use a feeler gauge between the
    valve lifter and camshaft.

22. **What are common starting faults?**

    Weak battery, corroded terminals, bad starter motor, faulty ignition switch,
    park/neutral switch failure, lack of fuel, lack of spark, poor compression,
    and timing-belt or timing-chain failure.

23. **What can cause engine overheating?**

    Low coolant, radiator leaks, a faulty thermostat, blocked radiator fins, or
    water-pump failure.

24. **What do exhaust-smoke colors indicate?**

    Black smoke indicates excess fuel or a rich mixture. Blue smoke indicates
    burning oil. White smoke indicates coolant leakage into the combustion
    chamber.

25. **What diagnostic tools are listed for engine faults?**

    Compression tester, vacuum gauge, multimeter, OBD-II scanner, and
    stethoscope.

## VMR01 - Vehicle Maintenance and Repair

Sources: Chapter 01 through Chapter 16 PowerPoints in
`modules/VMR01-vehicle-maintenance-and-repair/learning-materials/`.

### Important Questions and Answers

1. **How should a vehicle be supported after lifting?**

   Lift it at the designated jacking point and support it with correctly
   positioned jack stands. Do not rely on the jack alone.

2. **What should be done before lifting a vehicle?**

   Park on a level surface, apply the parking brake, and chock the wheels.

3. **What should be recorded during vehicle intake?**

   Record the customer and vehicle information, the requested work or complaint,
   and inspection details on the job card or intake form.

4. **What is the recommended basic vehicle-cleaning order?**

   Start with the interior. For the exterior, clean systematically and work from
   the upper areas downward.

5. **What is checked during an engine inspection?**

   Check belts, hoses, electrical components, fluid leaks, fluid condition,
   exhaust smoke, abnormal noises, engine performance, and fault information.

6. **What are the main A/C components?**

   Compressor, condenser, receiver/dryer or accumulator, expansion valve or
   orifice tube, evaporator, and blower fan.

7. **What does the A/C compressor do?**

   It compresses low-pressure, low-temperature refrigerant gas into
   high-pressure, high-temperature gas.

8. **What does the condenser do?**

   It releases heat and changes the refrigerant into a high-pressure liquid.

9. **What does the expansion valve or orifice tube do?**

   It controls refrigerant flow and causes a rapid pressure and temperature
   drop before the evaporator.

10. **What does the evaporator do?**

    It absorbs heat from cabin air. The refrigerant vaporizes and the air is
    cooled and dehumidified.

11. **What can cause weak A/C airflow?**

    A clogged cabin filter, failing blower motor, blocked duct or vent, or an
    evaporator problem.

12. **What does oily residue around an A/C component suggest?**

    It can indicate a refrigerant-oil leak.

13. **What are the main petrol fuel-system components?**

    Fuel tank, fuel pump, fuel lines, fuel filter, and a carburetor or fuel
    injectors.

14. **What does the petrol fuel filter do?**

    It removes dirt and contaminants before fuel reaches the engine.

15. **What are symptoms of a clogged petrol fuel filter?**

    Difficult starting, stalling, misfire or rough idle, loss of power, sluggish
    acceleration, and poor fuel economy.

16. **What does a diesel lift pump do?**

    It transfers low-pressure fuel from the tank to the high-pressure injection
    pump.

17. **Why must a diesel fuel filter remove water and dirt?**

    Clean, dry fuel protects the high-pressure pump and injectors from damage.

18. **What does a diesel high-pressure fuel pump do?**

    It raises fuel pressure to the level required for direct injection.

19. **What is a common-rail diesel system?**

    A high-pressure pump supplies a common rail, and electronically controlled
    injectors draw fuel from that rail.

20. **What does an ignition coil do?**

    It raises battery voltage to the high voltage required to produce a spark
    at the spark plug.

21. **What are two common steering-gear types?**

    Rack-and-pinion and recirculating-ball steering gears.

22. **What is the difference between hydraulic and electric power steering?**

    Hydraulic steering uses a pump and hydraulic pressure. Electric power
    steering uses an electric motor and electronic control.

23. **What does a clutch do?**

    It connects and disconnects the engine from the manual transmission so gears
    can be changed.

24. **What is the gear-ratio formula taught in the transmission material?**

    Gear ratio equals the number of teeth on the driven gear divided by the
    number of teeth on the driving gear.

25. **What does a torque converter do?**

    It transfers engine power to an automatic transmission through fluid and
    permits smooth take-off.

26. **What are the functions of automatic transmission fluid?**

    It transmits hydraulic pressure and power, lubricates, cools, and provides
    controlled friction for transmission operation.

27. **How does a hydraulic brake system operate?**

    Pressing the brake pedal moves the master cylinder. Hydraulic pressure is
    transmitted through brake fluid to apply the wheel brakes.

28. **What does ABS do?**

    It monitors wheel speed and controls brake pressure to prevent wheel lock
    during hard braking.

29. **What does a starter solenoid do?**

    It acts as a high-current relay and a mechanical actuator that engages the
    starter drive gear.

30. **What is OBD?**

    On-Board Diagnostics is the vehicle's self-diagnostic and reporting system.
    It gives access to subsystem status and stored fault information.

31. **What do the first letters of OBD-II trouble codes mean?**

    P means powertrain, B means body, C means chassis, and U means network.

32. **What does a differential do?**

    It transmits power while allowing wheels on the same axle to rotate at
    different speeds when turning.

33. **What is the difference between 4WD and AWD?**

    The slides describe 4WD as primarily intended for off-road traction and
    normally selected by the driver. AWD automatically distributes power for
    on-road traction and stability.

34. **What are the main suspension components?**

    Springs, shock absorbers, struts, control arms, sway bars, ball joints, and
    bushings.

35. **What does a shock absorber do?**

    It converts spring movement into heat and prevents uncontrolled bouncing.

36. **What is a strut?**

    It combines a coil spring and shock absorber in one structural assembly.

## Exam Answer Method

For procedure or fault questions, answer only with steps supported by the
relevant lecturer slide:

1. State the component or system.
2. State its function.
3. Explain its operation in order.
4. Identify the fault symptom.
5. Give the cause and rectification shown in the slides.
6. Include the safety precaution taught for that task.
