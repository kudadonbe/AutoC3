## 1. Fuel System

Source chapters:

- Chapter 06 - Undertake Petrol Fuel System

### Petrol Fuel System Diagram

```text
   v

   |
   v
Fuel Filter
   |
   v
Fuel Lines
   |
   v
Carburetor or Fuel Injectors
   |
   v
Engine Combustion Chamber
```

### Components and Functions

- **Fuel tank:** Stores petrol for the vehicle.
- **Fuel pump:** Draws fuel from the tank and pumps it under pressure to the
  engine.
- **Fuel filter:** Removes dirt and contaminants from the fuel before it reaches
  the engine.
- **Fuel lines:** Carry fuel from the tank to the engine components.
- **Carburetor:** Older system that mixes fuel and air mechanically.
- **Fuel injectors:** Modern electronically controlled nozzles that spray fuel
  into the engine.

### Fuel Flow Process

1. Fuel is stored in the fuel tank.
2. The fuel pump draws fuel from the tank.
3. Fuel passes through the fuel filter.
4. Clean fuel travels through fuel lines.
5. Fuel reaches the carburetor or fuel injectors.
6. The carburetor or injectors mix/deliver fuel for combustion.
7. The engine burns the air-fuel mixture to produce power.

### Diesel Fuel System

```text
Fuel Tank
   |
   v
Lift Pump / Transfer Pump
   |
   v
Diesel Fuel Filter / Water Separator
   |
   v
High-Pressure Fuel Pump
   |
   v
Common Rail / Injector Pump System
   |
   v
Injector Nozzles
   |
   v
Combustion Chamber
```

### Diesel Components and Functions

- **Fuel tank:** Stores diesel fuel.
- **Lift pump:** Transfers low-pressure fuel from the tank to the high-pressure
  injection pump.
- **Diesel fuel filter:** Removes contaminants, dirt, and water from diesel
  fuel.
- **High-pressure fuel pump:** Raises fuel pressure for injection.
- **Common rail:** Stores high-pressure fuel for electronically controlled
  injectors.
- **Injectors:** Atomize fuel into the combustion chamber.

```text
   |

   |

Disc Rotor / Brake Drum
   |
   v
Wheel Slows or Stops
```

### Components and Functions

- **Brake pedal:** Foot-operated lever pressed by the driver.
- **Brake booster/servo:** Assists braking force.
- **Master cylinder:** Creates hydraulic pressure.
- **Brake fluid:** Transfers pressure through the brake system.
- **Brake lines and hoses:** Carry brake fluid pressure to the wheels.
- **Caliper:** Presses brake pads against the disc rotor.
- **Brake pads:** Friction material used in disc brakes.
- **Disc/rotor:** Rotating part slowed by brake pads.
- **Brake drum:** Rotating part used in drum brakes.
- **Wheel cylinder:** Applies brake shoes in a drum brake system.
- **Parking brake:** Holds the vehicle stationary when parked.

### Working Principle

1. The driver presses the brake pedal.
2. The master cylinder creates hydraulic pressure.
3. Brake fluid carries pressure through brake lines.
4. The caliper or wheel cylinder applies the friction material.
5. Brake pads or shoes press against the disc or drum.
6. Friction changes vehicle kinetic energy into heat.
7. The wheel slows down or stops.

### ABS Working Principle

- Wheel speed sensors monitor wheel speed.
- The ABS ECU detects possible wheel lock.
- The hydraulic modulator reduces and reapplies brake pressure rapidly.
- ABS helps prevent wheel lock and helps the driver maintain steering control
  during hard braking.

## 3. Suspension System

Source chapter:

- Chapter 16 - Suspension System

### Purpose of Suspension

The suspension system connects the vehicle body to the wheels. Its purposes are:

- Support vehicle weight.
- Absorb road shocks and vibrations.
- Keep tires in contact with the road.
- Improve ride comfort.
- Improve handling, braking, and stability.

### Main Components

- Springs.
- Shock absorbers/dampers.
- Struts.
- Control arms/wishbones.
- Sway bars/anti-roll bars.
- Ball joints.
- Bushings.

### Independent Suspension

Independent suspension allows each wheel to move separately. If one wheel moves
over a bump, the opposite wheel is less affected.

Examples from the course material include:

- MacPherson strut.
- Double wishbone.
- Multi-link.

Advantages:

- Better ride comfort.
- Better handling.
- Better tire contact on uneven roads.
- Improved control during cornering.

### Dependent Suspension

Dependent suspension connects the wheels on the same axle. Movement of one wheel
can affect the other wheel.

Examples:

- Solid axle.
- Leaf spring suspension.

Advantages:

- Strong and durable.
- Useful for trucks, buses, and heavy-duty vehicles.
- Simpler construction.
- Good load carrying ability.

### Shock Absorber Function

A shock absorber controls spring movement. It converts movement energy into heat
and prevents the vehicle from bouncing uncontrollably.

### Strut Function

A strut combines a coil spring and shock absorber into one structural unit.

## 4. Steering System

Source chapter:

- Chapter 09 - Inspection and Service Steering System Components

### Purpose of Steering System

The steering system converts driver movement of the steering wheel into movement
of the road wheels. It provides direction control, stability, road feedback, and
driver comfort.

### Main Components

- Steering wheel.
- Steering column and shaft.
- Universal joints.
- Steering gear.
- Rack and pinion.
- Recirculating ball steering gear.
- Linkages.
- Tie rods.
- Steering arms.
- Steering knuckle.
- Power steering pump or electric motor.

### Power Steering

Power steering reduces the effort needed to turn the steering wheel.

Types:

- **Hydraulic power steering:** Uses a pump and hydraulic fluid pressure.
- **Electric power steering:** Uses an electric motor and electronic control.

### Steering Fluid

In hydraulic power steering, steering fluid transfers pressure from the pump to
assist steering movement. Low fluid or leaking fluid can make the steering heavy
and noisy.

### Common Inspection Points

- Steering free play.
- Steering fluid level and leaks.
- Noise when turning.
- Tie rod end wear.
- Ball joint and bushing condition.
- Steering rack boots.
- Uneven tire wear or alignment symptoms.

## 5. Drive Types

Source chapter:

- Chapter 14 - Service Final Drive Assembly Components

### Front-Wheel Drive (FWD)

In FWD, engine power goes to the front wheels. The front wheels steer and drive
the vehicle.

Advantages:

- Compact layout.
- Common in passenger cars.
- Good traction because engine weight is over the drive wheels.
- Usually lighter and more fuel efficient.

Disadvantages:

- Front tires do both steering and driving work.
- Less suitable for heavy-duty or high-performance load use compared with some
  other layouts.

### Rear-Wheel Drive (RWD)

In RWD, engine power goes to the rear wheels. The front wheels mainly steer.

Advantages:

- Better balance for handling.
- Good for performance and load-carrying applications.
- Front wheels are not responsible for driving force.

Disadvantages:

- More drivetrain parts such as propeller shaft and rear differential.
- Can have weaker traction on slippery roads compared with FWD if unloaded.

### Four-Wheel Drive (4WD)

4WD sends power to all four wheels. The course material describes it as mainly
for off-road or low-traction use and usually selected by the driver.

Modes:

- **2WD:** Normal dry road driving.
- **4H:** Slippery surfaces at normal speed.
- **4L:** Low-speed, high-torque off-road use.

Advantages:

- Strong traction off road.
- Useful in mud, sand, steep slopes, and poor road conditions.

Disadvantages:

- Heavier and more complex.
- Can use more fuel.
- Not always suitable for dry paved roads when locked.

### All-Wheel Drive (AWD)

AWD automatically sends power to all four wheels as needed for traction and
stability.

Advantages:

- Good on-road traction.
- Useful in rain, snow, ice, and light unpaved conditions.
- Usually works without driver selection.

Disadvantages:

- More complex than two-wheel drive.
- Can be heavier and use more fuel.
- Not designed for severe off-road use like traditional 4WD.
